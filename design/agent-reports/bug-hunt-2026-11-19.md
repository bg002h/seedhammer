## Recovery and address derivation

### C1 · Critical · New · A surplus CODEX32 share silently changes the recovered seed

`codex32/codex32.go:188`, `Interpolate`; `cmd/biptool/main.go:373`, `biptool interpolate`, also used by `derive`. Feed these four lines on stdin. The first three are the published A, C and D shares used by `TestInterpolate`. The fourth is a new E share generated with sixteen zero bytes and `biptool seed -threshold 3 -seedlen 16 -id cash -idx e`.

```text
ms13casha320zyxwvutsrqpnmlkjhgfedca2a8d0zehn8a0t
ms13cashcacdefghjklmnpqrstuvwxyz023949xq35my48dr
ms13cashd0wsedstcdcts64cd7wvy4m90lm28w4ffupqs7rm
ms13casheqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqqa8zxete8lyl02
```

The first three recover entropy `ffeeddccbbaa99887766554433221100`. Adding the published E, `ms13casheekgpemxzshcrmqhaydlp6yhms3ws7320xyxsar9`, recovers the same bytes. Substituting the zero-data E returns exit 0, empty stderr and `ms13cashskuq5jppqjsu6zaps4y06dh2r87hc0xg2kdyzghe`. Piping that result through `biptool derive seed` yields **`b7014904209439a17430a91fa6dd433f`**. A holder restoring from three good shares and one from another split receives a different wallet. The on-device picker at `gui/codex32_polish.go:172` stops at the threshold. The surplus-share result was reproduced in the library and CLI.

**Fix and regression.** Interpolate from exactly the threshold number of distinct shares. To preserve support for consistent extras, predict each extra at its own index and compare its full codeword before returning success. Reject duplicate or conflicting indices before the target-is-present shortcut. Two unshared `s` records with ID `test`, one generated from sixteen zero bytes and the other from sixteen `01` bytes, currently return whichever is listed first. Retain the supported single-secret identity operation. Test both E values above, reversed conflicting `s` inputs, and an exactly-threshold control. Require a named mismatch and zero output for the conflicting cases. The Rust primary implements the extra-share check in `mnemonic-secret/crates/ms-codec/src/shares.rs::combine`. [BIP 93 recovery and share generation](https://bips.dev/93/#recovering-secret)

### C2 · Critical · Prior lead reproduced · A public key silently derives a different receive address for a hardened suffix

`address/address.go:289`, `derivePubKey`, reached by `address.Receive` and `Change` after direct descriptor import. Use this synthetic signet master public key as `K` throughout the report.

```text
tpubD6NzVbkrYhZ4Wgf68pWWfTRzdXMoepBvepfiAKtNoE7RvbAbWVfWzQxH1jbkfNk3iJ9zR65Yw6u3B2QZzpkMSTN4y8Lfm1t44HbpZX7efhZ
```

Parse `wpkh(K/0h/*)` with `bip380.Parse`, then call `address.Receive(d,0)`. Expected a hardened-public-derivation refusal. Observed **`tb1qnskw4kf6qevrcyjjd7cnn39uemz9j2cnaxa4u3`**, nil error, exactly the address of `wpkh(K/0/*)`. Likewise `/*h` and `/*` both produced `tb1qhdgyak30llluz36r6lk5ywup5uq0jz0ejkztjl`. A holder can fund an address their specified descriptor will never derive. The companion's stricter host admission already documents this case in `SPEC_descriptor_input.md` §7. The October fork hunt listed it under “Not checked.”

**Fix and regression.** Return an unsupported-derivation error before deriving any hardened use-site child from an xpub. Preserve hardened key *origin* components, which record how the xpub was obtained. Make `Supported` and the `resolveChild` comparison agree with the address router's refusal. Assert no address for the two hardened suffixes on both chains. Keep the two literal ordinary receive addresses above as controls. [BIP 32 public child derivation](https://bips.dev/32/#public-parent-key--public-child-key), [BIP 380 key normalization](https://bips.dev/380/#normalization-of-key-expressions-with-hardened-derivation)

### I1 · Important · New · An ambiguous mnemonic prefix selects one of two valid wallets

`bip39/bip39.go:312`, `Parse`, also reached from the NFC scanner at `gui/scan.go:80` and sealed-record admission at `seal/record.go:261`. Enter **`abs`, then ten copies of `abandon`, then `bargain`**. Both complete versions, with first word `absent` and `absorb`, independently pass the checksum. The abbreviated version returned `absent` without an ambiguity error. Both the real host-side scanner state machine and `seal.AdmitSection(..., seal.SectionEncrypted)` accepted it. Someone transcribing the `absorb` wallet would restore the other one.

**Fix and regression.** Accept exact words first, including short complete words. For abbreviated tokens, accept only a unique word-list prefix. The adjacent sorted entry suffices to detect a second match after `ClosestWord`. Require the abbreviated case to fail before admission and both full phrases to succeed and differ. Keep a unique four-letter abbreviation as a positive control. [BIP 39 English word-list criteria](https://bips.dev/39/#wordlist)

### I2 · Important · New · A three-word fragment is admitted as a BIP-39 seed

`bip39/bip39.go:117`, `Mnemonic.Valid`, reached by both mnemonic parsers, the scanner and sealed admission. `bip39.Parse([]byte("abandon abandon ability"))` succeeded and yielded **four bytes** of entropy. The scanner returned a mnemonic, and encrypted-section admission returned one record and nil error. The same length with final word `abandon` failed its checksum. This demonstrates a checksum over an unsupported size. A holder can mistake an incomplete record for a recoverable seed.

**Fix and regression.** Validate the five legal word counts and the bounds of every word index before checksum arithmetic. Assert refusal of the exact three-word record through `Valid`, parsing and sealed admission, and cover 6 and 9 words. Retain independent 12/15/18/21/24-word controls. October #12 covered the empty and overlong panic cases. Its proposed upper-bound fix alone would still admit this input. [BIP 39 entropy and word counts](https://bips.dev/39/#generating-the-mnemonic)

### I3 · Important · New · A misplaced derivation option returns the master xpub

`cmd/biptool/main.go`, `run` and `derive` (format dispatch near line 174). Make the stdin control with bytes `00..0f` and `biptool seed -id test -seedlen 16`. On that input, `biptool derive -path m/83696968h/32h/0h xpub` returned the child ending in `cLhwaALp6H`. Move the same option after the format, `biptool derive xpub -path m/83696968h/32h/0h`, and it exits 0, returning the **master** ending in `W1EGMcet8`. Plain `derive xpub` gives the same master. This can put the wrong key into a watch-only backup.

**Fix and regression.** Validate leftover arguments at each command and format, including formats with no options. Reject a misplaced flag with usage and empty stdout or explicitly implement it in that position. Test both spellings above against the public control and require that a requested path never silently resolves to the root.

### I4 · Important · Prior #28 with a new silent case · BIP-39 export can add entropy that was never supplied

`cmd/biptool/main.go:191`, `biptool derive bip39`. Feed 31 zero bytes through `biptool seed -id test -seedlen 31`, then `biptool derive bip39 -words 24`. Both commands exit 0. The result is 23 copies of `abandon` followed by `art`, identical to the 32-zero-byte control. The same happened for 19→20, 23→24 and 27→28 bytes. The decoded slice has spare capacity, so slicing to the requested size can expose a zero byte. October #28 and `sh-biptool-short-seed` cover a loud failure from this line. The silent result raises the impact for the host tool.

**Fix and regression.** Compare the requested entropy length with `len(seed)` before slicing. Refuse every insufficient input regardless of slice capacity, retain the supported BIP-85 truncation from longer entropy, and validate signed length options before slicing. Exercise 31 and 32 bytes through the CLI, require nonzero/empty output for 31, and retain the old no-seed row.

## File delivery, transport and tooling

### I5 · Important · New · `biptool` reports success when the backup write fails

`cmd/biptool/main.go:192,207,216,318,394`, the formatted derivation outputs, raw seed writer, seed encoder and share interpolator. With the I3 input, ordinary `derive seed` delivers 16 bytes and `derive xpub` delivers 112 including the newline. Redirect stdout to Linux `/dev/full`. Both exit 0 with empty stderr, as do `seed -id test -seedlen 16` with 16 input bytes and `interpolate` with a valid secret. The same binary's `rand -n 16` correctly exits 2 with a no-space error. A pipeline can mark an undelivered backup complete.

**Fix and regression.** Return the error from every payload `Write` or formatted print, and detect short writes where appropriate. Preserve success diagnostics only after delivery. Test `run` with a writer that accepts a prefix and fails, plus a subprocess stdout-failure row for each distinct writer. Assert a nonzero exit and retain the valid output controls.

### I6 · Important · New · `picosign hash` and `extract` lose output errors

`cmd/picosign/main.go:90,115`, `extract` and `hash`. Both return success with stdout on `/dev/full`. On a writable destination, the same input returns 129 bytes of signature text including newline, or a 32-byte binary digest. A build step can report that an image digest or signature was exported while delivering nothing.

**Reproduction and regression.** Wrap the decompressed `picobin/testdata/signed.bin.gz` fixture in sequential 256-byte UF2 payloads. Use the header and footer from `uf2/testdata/2blocks.uf2`, address `0x10000000 + 256*i`, zero-based block numbers and the final block count. Zero-pad the final payload. The control digest in hex is `15cf016da39866e8d1c0dff1aaa29fd0429876f9b55a290c1fc6fce819783557`. Run `picosign hash FILE` and `picosign extract FILE` with each destination. Propagate both write results to `main`. Assert successful reference bytes and nonzero exits for failed delivery.

### I7 · Important · Prior lead reproduced · A truncated UF2 reads as complete

`uf2/uf2.go:67,101`, `Reader.Read` and `loadBlock`. Use `uf2/testdata/2blocks.uf2`, explicitly set each header's little-endian block number at offset 20 to 0 or 1 and its total at offset 24 to 2. Call `io.ReadAll(uf2.NewReader(..., uf2.FamilyRP2350ARMSigned))`. The complete 1,024-byte control returned 512 payload bytes and nil error. The first 512 bytes returned 256 and **nil**. The first 32 bytes, just a header promising a payload, returned zero and **nil**. Consumers cannot tell that declared firmware bytes are missing. October's “Not checked” list mentioned the block-boundary case.

**Fix and regression.** Track completion of each physical block and the declared file block sequence, including family-filtered blocks. Convert premature EOF during a header, payload or footer to a truncation error. Permit clean EOF at an actual file boundary. Keep the complete control and assert an error at both cut points. Downstream `picobin` may catch some truncations independently.

### I8 · Important · New · An NDEF URI changes with the caller's read buffer

`nfc/ndef/ndef.go:202`, `NewRecordReader(...).Read`. Decode hex `d1 01 02 55 04 61`, an ordinary URI record with an HTTPS prefix and body `a`. Repeated reads to EOF should yield `https://a` at every buffer size. A 16-byte buffer did. Four bytes yielded `httpa` and one byte yielded **`ha`**, both with normal EOF. The code copies as much of the expanded prefix as fits, then advances to the payload.

**Fix and regression.** Retain the pending prefix and its offset across calls. Decode the same fixed record with buffer lengths 1 through the longest supported prefix. Assert byte-for-byte equality with the large-buffer result. October #18 concerns empty records. This one has a nonempty payload and loses data on an ordinary reader call.

### I9 · Important · New · A final read carrying both data and EOF can expose a skipped TLV

`nfc/ndef/ndef.go:36`, `NewMessageReader(...).Read`. A non-NDEF TLV `fd 03 61 62 63` yielded an empty result and nil error over `bytes.NewReader`. Simply wrap the same underlying reader with Go's `testing/iotest.DataErrReader`. `io.ReadAll` now returns **`abc` plus `ndef: tlv: EOF`**. A real NDEF TLV `03 03 61 62 63` returns `abc` and nil on the first reader, but `abc` plus that error on the wrapper. Both underlying read conventions are legal. This can discard a valid last message or expose bytes from a container the API promises to skip.

**Fix and regression.** Account for the returned bytes and the skip flag before handling EOF. A completed permitted TLV should deliver its bytes with ordinary end-of-stream semantics. A skipped one must expose zero bytes. Report unexpected EOF when the declared length is still unmet. Exercise both five-byte fixtures through both readers.

### I10 · Important · Prior lead reproduced · An mk1 encoder accepts contradictory network metadata

`mk/encode.go:39,75`, `mk.Encode` and `encodeBytecode`. Construct a `mk.Card` with `Xpub: K` from C2, `Path: "m"`, one stub `[4]byte{1,2,3,4}` and `Network: "mainnet"`. Both `Encode` and `Decode` succeed. The decoded card says **testnet**. With `Network: "testnet"` it round-trips normally. This breaks the public encoder's stated round-trip guarantee. The conflicting input can produce an inspectable card labeled for a different network. The October report listed this as an unchecked suspicion.

**Fix and regression.** Use the extended key's version as the wire authority, as the Rust primary does in `mk-codec/src/bytecode/xpub_compact.rs`, and reject an explicitly contradictory network before emitting chunks. Preserve supported call sites that leave the redundant field empty. Cover the signet/testnet-version control, the conflict above and a mainnet-version control.

### I12 · Important · New on the installation pages · Literal image placeholders are shell redirections

`README.md:167` and `docs/custom-firmware.md:370,389,399`. The printed command `picotool load --verify <image>.signed.uf2` failed before invocation in an empty directory with `image: No such file or directory`. ### I13 · Important for public-library callers · Prior FOLLOWUP reproduced · Equivalent passphrases derive different seeds

`bip39/bip39.go:261`, `MnemonicSeed`. Take the published eleven-`abandon` plus `about` mnemonic. Use passphrases `"caf\u00e9"` and `"cafe\u0301"`. Compare both spellings with the independent normalized result and retain the published ASCII vectors. The finding does not require changing the device keyboard. [BIP 39 mnemonic-to-seed conversion](https://bips.dev/39/#from-mnemonic-to-seed)
